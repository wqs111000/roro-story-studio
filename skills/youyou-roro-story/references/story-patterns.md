# Original story patterns

Use these as structural tools, not as source texts.

## Reliable patterns for age four

- **Three gentle attempts:** Two attempts reveal information; the third combines what was learned.
- **Cumulative journey:** Each new friend contributes one sound, object, or idea that returns near the ending.
- **Lost and found:** A misplaced object travels through a safe environment and helps characters notice where things belong.
- **Small helper:** A physically small character solves one important part without proving that bigger characters are foolish.
- **Mistaken monster:** A frightening sound or shadow receives a concrete, reassuring explanation.
- **Choice of two paths:** Both options are safe; they reveal different observations and reconverge quickly.
- **Preparation ritual:** A repeated sequence such as check, pack, wave, and go supports habits without lecturing.
- **Return home:** An adventure concludes with a calm return, a remembered detail, and emotional security.

## What may be learned from popular picture books

It is acceptable to study general craft features: page-turn anticipation, repeated refrains, child participation, visual contrast, comic reversal, predictable rhythm, and emotional reassurance. Create a fresh combination with original characters, scenes, language, and images.

Do not request or reproduce a recognizable style of a living illustrator. Describe an original visual treatment through medium, shapes, palette, lighting, composition, and mood.
